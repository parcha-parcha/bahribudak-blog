# Stenter Exhaust Cleaning and Fire Prevention

Deposits of lint, oil smoke, wax, finishing chemicals and sticky aerosols in stenter exhaust ducts can reduce airflow, increase fan load and create combustible deposits that may help a fire spread through the duct system.

This R04 revision treats exhaust cleaning as a fire-prevention and production-continuity control, not as a delayed maintenance activity. Cleaning intervals must be confirmed by the machine maker's instructions, fabric type, chemical load, operating hours, filter pressure differential, fan airflow, access-port observations and actual deposit data.

## Main decision

Cleaning frequency is not a fixed recipe. It must be managed through site evidence, documented inspection and technical approval.

## Technical scope

- Stenter exhaust ducts, stacks and roof penetrations
- Filter, ESP, heat-recovery and CIP areas
- Fans, motors, bearings, dampers and airflow indicators
- Access-port inspection system
- Hot-work permit control
- Abnormal odour, smoke, ember/spark signs and emergency shutdown
- Post-incident restart approval

## Fire-risk chain

1. Fabric, finish, silicone, resin, wax, oil and contaminants are carried into the exhaust system by hot air.
2. Low airflow and duct geometry increase aerosol and particle deposition.
3. Lint, oil and wax deposits accumulate at elbows, horizontal runs, fan housings, filters and roof penetrations.
4. Excess temperature, hot surfaces, electrical faults, friction or hot work can initiate ignition.
5. Combustible deposits and fan-induced airflow can support fire spread through the duct route.

## Risk-based decision levels

| Level | Typical indicators | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Low oil/finish load and stable pressure differential | Weekly visual check plus monthly access-port inspection; maintain OEM requirements. |
| Medium | Wax/silicone use, increasing odour or rapid filter loading | Weekly access-port check, monthly photographed inspection and three-month cleaning review. |
| High | Heavy oil/wax aerosol, recurring filter blockage or airflow loss | Daily critical-indicator check, weekly detailed inspection and fixed production cleaning windows. |
| Critical | Smoke, ember/spark signs, abnormal temperature, fan failure or post-hot-work concern | Stop the line, isolate energy, apply the emergency procedure and require technical restart approval. |

## Hot-work rule

Welding, grinding or cutting near a stenter duct, fan, stack or roof penetration must not start before deposits are removed, energy is isolated, a hot-work permit is issued, a fire watch is assigned and the close-out inspection is completed.

## Emergency principle

Do not open a suspected burning duct and do not apply unauthorised water or chemicals. Bring the line to a safe stop, isolate electrical/heat/gas energy, inform the emergency team and do not restart without a documented technical approval.

This publication does not replace the equipment manufacturer's instructions, local regulation, insurance requirements or a site-specific fire-risk assessment. Final cleaning intervals and controls must be validated for the actual plant conditions.
